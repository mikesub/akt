# Agent instructions

Personal automation for Artemy Troitsky's programs: read each episode's tracklist with `claude`, find the tracks, publish a static site. One JSON file per program, one static page, run by hand on the laptop.

His programs, oldest first (the page lists them all under «Что есть в архиве», "нет данных" for one with no data):

- «Ковчег дядюшки Ко» (1990–1996): kept for the record; playlists are unlikely to exist. No filter.
- «FM Достоевский» (`fmd`, 1997 – 27 Jan 2013, numbered #1–#820): 775 playlists, built offline from the sources in `programs/fmd/sources/` by `node bin/fmd-playlists.js` (never edit them by hand: a rebuild overwrites them). `programs/fmd/NOTES.md` has every source, how far to trust it, coverage and gaps; `programs/fmd/CROSSCHECK.md` where sources disagree; `programs/fmd/MISSING.md` the 45 episodes no source has. Its last years were on Финам FM, which kept its own count: Finam's №1 (05.09.2010) is #702, №119 (27.01.2013, «PLAYLIST #820 (№119 по нумерации Финам.fm)») the last episode. 95 playlists have no `date:` (#1–#66, #78–#102, #110–#114, which no source dates): no date is estimated or shown for them; the year check uses the year of the next dated episode by number, which they aired no later than (`airedBy` in `src/pipeline.js`).
- «Stereo Voodoo» (`voodoo`, Mar 2013 – Dec 2015): all 131 playlists. Its podfm.ru feed is gone; they come from Apple's copy of it (iTunes lookup, `id=1062986266&entity=podcastEpisode`), each episode description being its playlist, minus the footer links. Dates are Moscow time (Apple stores UTC, a day early for midnight releases).
- «Песни и пляски» (`pesni`, Feb 2016 – Jul 2017): 64 playlists; #29 is missing (the page names missing numbers from the data). Same source as Stereo Voodoo (iTunes lookup, `id=1084653395`), the tracklist being the description after its «Трэк лист» line; formats vary (unnumbered at first, then "1.", "1", "01"). Dates are upload dates in Moscow time, not air dates: several episodes were uploaded on one day.
- «Записки из подполья» (`zapiski`, Feb 2016 – Jul 2017): all 66 playlists. Same source and handling as «Песни и пляски» (iTunes lookup, `id=1084653585`); upload dates, not air dates.
- «Музыка на Свободе» on Radio Svoboda (`svoboda`, Jan 2017 – Feb 2025): playlists from the end of each episode's text article on svoboda.org (see Svoboda below).
- «Стереоплан Троицкого», a podcast (`stereoplan`, since Jan 2025): `node bin/stereoplan.js`, the tracklists being the feed's episode descriptions.

## Where things stand (2026-10-16)

On the site: every program, «FM Достоевский» since 2026-10-16 (#701, 5 songs in an hour, waits for the owner's listen). The program folders are in `programs/<id>/`. Take the rest in this order.

1. The XLS's Russian names (FMD's early playlists give them in Latin letters, transliterated or translated) are restored in `titles.json`, each from the artist's recordings on MusicBrainz, the archive, or the owner's word. The owner's rule: the page shows the real title, in Cyrillic; an English title stays only if the artist really released it (ZGA, Terem Quartet, Zvuki Mu's «Honey Baby»). Most were found on Discogs from the releases the owner pointed to (the XLS's label and album lead there). The later sources (fmd.soyuz.ru, Финам) also print some Russian names in Latin letters: those are restored too (#426–#454, #521, #603); the rest of the 69 Russian-marked Latin entries there are the artists' own Latin releases (Netslov, Verba, Nehit, Starostin's «Zhyli-Byli», Oyme, Messer Chups, Kim & Buran, …), kept as printed. Still open, left for later by the owner, as printed: #13 Marina Nicolay «Girl From Amsterdam», #23 Zolotaya Dolina «Dima, Wave Hand To Your Mother», #32 Tambourine «Music Box # 7», #50 Zima «Freezed» («Trance Siberia Express»; not MusicBrainz's black-metal Зима), #87 The Best Of Suicide «-30» («7Б Compilation»: the line looks garbled). A full `--misses` pass gave nothing new a day after the first lookups; after a fix to the matching, ask again only the tracks it touches (`--misses --only <pattern>`). Then the page's line for FMD can show #1's exact date, once a source has it (it says «1997» until then: `started` in `PROGRAMS`).
2. Countries: done, in the data and on the page (since 2026-10-16). Each track has `printed_country` (as printed, read by `claude`; `node bin/countries.js` read it for tracks parsed before the parse did) and `country` (codes from `countries.json`: ISO 3166-1, GB-WLS/GB-NIR, former states as SU/CS/YU/DD/VD, a printed "Europe" as EU, regions as their country, no code for peoples or regions across borders such as Kurdistan or Tibet). 20,420 of 21,783 tracks print one. One that prints none, or only a vague one ("Europe", a placeholder such as "?": `vague` in `src/countries.js`), gets, by `bin/countries.js`, the codes its artist has most often where the archive prints one (`country_from: archive`, 487), else with `--musicbrainz` its credited artists' countries (`country_from: musicbrainz`, 581; through the recording already found, else by a name only one artist has), asked once; a printed country always wins. 21,424 of 21,783 tracks have a country (MusicBrainz found none for 295). On the page: «Страна», below the decades and folded by default, a chip per flag, so a former state shares its successor's (🇷🇺 «Россия/СССР», 🇨🇿, 🇷🇸, 🇩🇪, 🇻🇳, 🇬🇧 with Northern Ireland; the data and the table keep them apart), most tracks first; no chip for a printed "Europe" (EU: such a track goes with no country in the filter, its 🇪🇺 still in the column; a «Европа» chip for every European country was tried and dropped, the owner's call), 🏴‍☠️ (no country) last; several can be picked (a track matches with any of its countries), unlike the decades (one at a time). A column of every track's flags before «Эпизод» (on a phone, beside the year), its name on hover, and for an inferred one where it comes from. `?country=GB,US`, `unknown` for none; the CSV's `country` holds the codes, space-separated. Names from the browser's `Intl.DisplayNames('ru')`, except where it is wrong or has none: SU СССР, CS Чехословакия, YU Югославия, DD ГДР, VD Южный Вьетнам, GB-WLS Уэльс, GB-NIR Северная Ирландия. Emoji flags from the code; a former state shows its successor's flag (the owner's call): SU 🇷🇺, CS 🇨🇿, YU 🇷🇸, DD 🇩🇪, VD 🇻🇳, GB-NIR 🇬🇧, GB-WLS its own 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿. Labels later, the same way. And track lengths: Apple's lookup results carry `trackTimeMillis`, which the pipeline does not keep; the batch lookup by id that fills the album and artist links (`findAppleLinks`) could store it too, new lookups saving it as they go. It is the length of Apple's version (an album cut, a remaster), not always the one aired; MusicBrainz has recording lengths as a second opinion. On the page: the total playing time of the tracks the filters leave. Neither is for now. If a pass needs much more of MusicBrainz than its one request a second allows, a local copy from its CC0 dumps (`musicbrainz-docker` on a rented VM, deleted after) is the way; ask first, it is a hosted service.
3. After everything is parsed: Apple Music playlists per decade, once (e.g. «Троицкий: 1960-е», each track once, only those with an Apple Music link). There is no free official way: the Apple Music API needs a paid developer membership, which the owner won't buy. So it uses the web player's tokens: the owner copies the `authorization` and `media-user-token` request headers from music.apple.com into `.env` (never committed, never pasted into a chat), and a one-off script outside the repo creates the playlists in the owner's library, through `src/http.js` (which needs POST and headers for it). Unofficial and against Apple's terms, and the tokens rotate: a one-off, not a feature. Still to decide with the owner: which programs, the names, the order (by year or by air date), and whether the tracks without a year get a playlist.

## What this is not

Not a product. No multi-user, no auth, no server, no re-hosting audio. If a change needs a backend or a hosted service, stop and ask.

## Hard boundaries

- Sources: every program keeps its raw playlists in the repo, so nothing depends on a source staying online. Every program but Stereoplan keeps one text file per playlist, `programs/<id>/playlists/<number>.txt` (`src/playlists.js`: a few header lines, `url:`, `date:`, `title:`, `note:`, a blank line, then the playlist as its source printed it; `<YYYY-MM-DD>.txt` for a special outside the numbering); a new program needs only its playlist files. Stereoplan's are `programs/stereoplan/descriptions/<number>.html`, each episode's description exactly as the feed publishes it (playlist and the host's notes interleaved; rewritten when it changes upstream).
- Pipeline: one, `src/pipeline.js`, for every program: `node bin/playlists.js <id>`; `bin/svoboda.js` is the same run for `svoboda`, plus `--fetch`; `bin/stereoplan.js` fetches the feed and saves the descriptions first. Idempotent and resumable: a playlist is parsed again only when its text changes (tracked by `playlist_hash`) or when asked with `--episode <n>`, and a track is looked up until every source has answered. A failed parse or lookup keeps what was there and is retried on the next run; it never blocks the others. `--parse-only` parses without lookups; `--misses`, `--musicbrainz` and `--relink` ask the sources again.
- Runs: a program's first run takes hours (parsing about 7 s a playlist, lookups 4–10 s a track, side by side); the owner runs it in a terminal (a background shell here stops at 2 h), in a retry loop, since MusicBrainz answers HTTP 503 when busy and a failed lookup waits for the next run. The lookups are paced per process, so never two lookup runs side by side; `--parse-only` makes no requests and can run beside one.
- Data: `docs/data/<id>.json` is each program's state. The playlist files (Stereoplan's feed) own every episode field; `tracks` comes from `claude` and the lookups. Keyed by episode number (Stereoplan's by guid), written with stable key order and episode order, so its git diff is the regression check. Never edited by hand.
- Svoboda: its podcast feed has no tracklists, and the article list is not crawlable (robots.txt disallows `?p=` paging), so `programs/svoboda/articles.txt` lists the articles by hand. `bin/svoboda.js --fetch` writes each playlist as `programs/svoboda/playlists/<number>.txt` (the playlist text only, never the commentary; a two-part article has two) and what it could not get to `programs/svoboda/MISSING.md`; a playlist found elsewhere is added by hand as such a file.
- Corrections: `overrides.json` (repo root) holds hand fixes for what no source gets right, such as a song Apple only has on a reissue. Each entry names an artist plus a track and/or album, the fields to set, and `why` with the source. The pipeline applies it on every run, after the lookups; never fix a data file by hand. `titles.json` (repo root) restores names a playlist prints in another form (the XLS's Russian names in Latin letters): each entry names an artist and track as printed, `restore` (the artist, track and/or album to show, each one the artist really released, as on MusicBrainz or elsewhere in the archive, never written from memory) and `why`. Applied before the lookups (`src/titles.js`), so they search for the restored names.
- Years: a track's year is its recording's first release (single, EP or album), never a reissue's, compilation's or remaster's. The pipeline discards any looked-up year later than the episode's air year (a later release's, never the track's) and looks such a track up again, so a stored year is never after its episode; a new source must do the same. A year no source gets right goes in `overrides.json`, with the release and its source in `why`.
- Site: static files in `docs/`, served by GitHub Pages from `main` at https://akt.mksb.me/ (`docs/CNAME`). No build step, no bundler. The page loads every program's data file (`PROGRAMS` in `docs/index.html`; one shows up with its episode menu once it has parsed episodes) and does all search and filtering in the browser: a decade filter and a country filter across all programs, an episode filter per program, a search box; the view is in the URL to share (`?q=…&decade=1980&country=GB,US&pesni=12&link=1&sort=-year`), each change a history entry; «Скачать CSV» downloads the rows the table shows (every track when nothing is filtered), built in the browser. No runtime API calls. Publishing is committing `docs/` and pushing `main`.
- LLM: the local `claude` CLI, driven only through `src/llm.js`: print mode, `--json-schema`, no tools, no MCP, cwd outside the repo. It reads the tracklist; it never invents a value the description does not contain.
- HTTP: every outgoing request goes through `src/http.js`: a minimum gap per host from that service's published limits, `Retry-After` and rate-limit headers honoured, 429/503 and network errors retried. A new service gets its gap there, with where the limit comes from.
- No audio processing. Tracks come from the playlists only; the site links to each episode's own page (Stereoplan's on mave, from the feed's `<link>`), never re-hosting its audio.
- Episode numbers: Stereoplan's feed metadata numbers are unreliable, so its episodes are keyed by RSS `guid` (UUIDs in this feed) and numbered from their titles. Every other program's are keyed by their playlist file's number (Svoboda's from its playlist header, "(выпуск 141)", "#356").
- Secrets: none are needed (the `claude` CLI uses its own login). If one ever is: env / `.env` only, never committed.
- Privacy: never send or write the owner's email or any other personal contact detail: not in User-Agent or other request headers, URLs, payloads, code, config, commits or files. No repo URL either: a User-Agent is the runtime's default or just `akt/0.1`. The project contact address in CLAUDE.md is the one exception, in two places only: MusicBrainz's User-Agent (it throttles one without a contact as anonymous), and the site's contact line for corrections and missing playlists. The footer's first line also links the owner's Bluesky profile after that address, at the owner's request (not in the header's contact line). If another service truly needs a contact, ask first.

## Working rules

- Data changelog: `CHANGELOG.md` (repo root, not on the page yet) tells whoever keeps a copy of the data whether to download it again. A change to the data's format (a field added, renamed or removed: **Format**, and **Breaking** if old readers can break), to its license, or to much of it at once (a program added, a pass over every track) gets an entry, dated, newest first, in the same commit. Routine updates (a new episode, a single fix) don't.
- Data license: the data files, `overrides.json`, `titles.json` and `countries.json` are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (CC0 until 2026-10-16), so that MusicBrainz's genres (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, whose ShareAlike allows later versions) can go in: per recording, else the album's, else the artist's, several a track, MusicBrainz's genre names as they are (its genres, not its free tags). The page credits MusicBrainz for them once they are in. Nothing under a license stricter than that (no commercial-use or no-derivatives terms). The playlists themselves are Troitsky's, under no license of ours.
- No runtime dependencies: Node stdlib only. Justify any new one in the commit message.
- Russian content is displayed as-is, and so is everything else from the sources (titles, artists, albums). The page's own interface is in Russian. Don't translate the host's notes.
- Work directly on `main`: no feature branches, no worktrees. Push to `origin/main` right after every commit.

## When unsure

Ask a one-line question with the two options you'd pick between. Don't build both.
